**Headline:** Spain lost its pro team. Its players led the AFLE in tackles and sacks anyway

**Subhead:** A census of Spaniards abroad in 2026 finds at least 27 on rosters outside Spain, from Marshall's record-setting kicker to seven players on the two Gold Bowl teams.

When European professional football split into two leagues in January, the Madrid Bravos were left without a place and stopped competing. Spain, which had two franchises in the ELF not long ago, began 2026 with none.

Ten months later, at least 27 Spanish players have been on a roster outside Spain this year: four in US college football, one at the NFL Academy and 22 in European leagues. That is the count of a census published by Primer Down, an independent outlet covering American football in Spain, built player by player from what leagues, clubs and universities publish.

**The Bravos diaspora**

Most of the 22 in Europe are there for one reason. In March the AFLE declared Spain "homegrown territory" for all its franchises: each could sign up to three Spaniards from the Bravos roster without them counting as imports, for 2026 only.

It went better than anyone could have expected. In the last leaders table the league published, after week 12, the tackles leader was a Spaniard, Berlin Thunder safety Pablo de Diego, with 81. The sacks leader was another, Rhein Fire defensive end Diego Paz, with 7.5. Second in sacks was a third, Vienna Vikings defensive end Alejandro "Iceman" Fernández, with 7. Paz, who came in as an import rather than through the quota, finished the year as the AFLE's Most Improved Defensive Player.

The Gold Bowl on 6 September in Duisburg, which Vienna won 68-5 over the Panthers Wrocław, had Spaniards on both sides. Fernández, safety Pablo Araya and guard David Culebras were with the Vikings. Receivers Eduardo Pérez and Juan Flores-Calderón, linebacker Jorge Hernández and quarterback Diego Lliteras were with Wrocław; Lliteras had won the Spanish league with Las Rozas Black Demons days before signing.

The rest of the AFLE list: Andy Vera and Carlos España (Berlin Thunder), Elvys Núñez and Celestin Ngimbi (Paris Lights), Aaron David, José Soares and Gabriel Rodríguez (Alpine Rams), and Yago Sánchez, Marcos Sánchez González and Nacho Diz (Firenze Red Lions). Offensive lineman Mikel Gómez Barroso signed with the Dresden Monarchs of Germany's GFL in April and later played for the Alpine Rams.

Outside the AFLE, international tight end Adrià Botella played for the Frankfurt Galaxy in the EFA, and the GFL champion Dresden Monarchs signed receiver Agustín Do Eyo from L'Hospitalet Pioners.

**Three kickers and a lineman**

In US college football the census found four Spaniards, and three of them kick. Jorge Díaz Nicolás, a redshirt freshman from Madrid, became the first player in Marshall's history to make five field goals in a game, going 5-for-5 in a 36-23 win over Old Dominion. Alejandro De La Poza, from Palma, was the UAC Special Teams Player of the Week in September after a game-winning field goal for Tarleton State at McNeese. Nico Cavanillas Alti kicks for Coastal Carolina, and Vito Moriana-Sigel, a fifth-year offensive lineman from Estepona, plays at Western Carolina.

One step before college is Tom Gabarró, a 19-year-old from Barcelona and the only Spaniard among the NFL Academy's 70 players at Loughborough.

**None in the NFL, yet**

No Spaniard plays in the NFL. The International Player Pathway's Class of 2026 includes none, and Paz, who worked out for the New England Patriots in October, was still unsigned as of 16 November.

**Why "at least"**

The census only counts players that an official source or a news outlet identifies as Spanish and who were on a roster in 2026. Junior colleges, some 270 small D3 and NAIA programmes and several European leagues could not be checked in depth, and four players are left out until their details are confirmed. For four of the AFLE signings, the number of games played is still unknown.
